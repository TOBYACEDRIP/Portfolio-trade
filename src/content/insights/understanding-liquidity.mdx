If market structure tells you *how* price is moving, liquidity helps explain *where* it's likely to go next. It's one of the most important concepts in my process — and one of the most misunderstood.

## What we mean by liquidity

In this context, liquidity means **resting orders**: stop-losses, pending entries and breakout orders that sit at predictable places on the chart. For a large order to be filled, there needs to be enough opposing volume. Areas packed with resting orders provide exactly that.

The most common places liquidity builds up:

- **Above old highs** — buy-side liquidity: stops from short sellers and buy-stop entries from breakout traders.
- **Below old lows** — sell-side liquidity: stops from buyers and sell-stop entries.
- **Equal highs and equal lows** — when price forms two or more highs or lows at almost the same level, it advertises a cluster of stops.
- **Trendline touches** — the more obvious the line, the more orders are likely placed around it.

## Draw on liquidity

At any moment I want to answer a simple question: **what is price most likely reaching for?** That target is the *draw on liquidity*.

On the higher timeframe it might be a previous week's high, an obvious set of equal lows, or an unfilled imbalance. Knowing the draw gives you two things:

1. A directional bias — price tends to move towards liquidity.
2. A logical target — if you're in a trade, opposing liquidity is a natural place to take profit.

## The liquidity sweep

A **liquidity sweep** happens when price runs through one of those pools and then fails to continue. The stops are triggered, the orders are filled — and the move reverses.

What makes a sweep meaningful:

- It happens **at a point of interest**, ideally a higher-timeframe one.
- It's followed by a **Market Structure Shift** or **Change in State of Delivery** in the opposite direction.
- The reversal shows **displacement** — strong, decisive candles rather than a slow drift.

Without those, a sweep is just a wick.

> Liquidity tells you where the fuel is. Structure and displacement tell you whether the engine has actually turned over.

## Using liquidity in the TOBYACE Method

Liquidity appears at every stage of the framework:

- **Top-Down Analysis** — identify the higher-timeframe draw and where external liquidity rests.
- **POI Logic** — prefer points of interest that sit just beyond a pool of liquidity, so a sweep can occur *into* the area.
- **Entry Model** — wait for the sweep, then the shift, then the retracement into the imbalance left behind.
- **Refinements** — place your stop beyond the sweep, and target the opposing liquidity pool.

## Where traders go wrong

**Placing stops exactly where everyone else does.** If your stop sits a few pips beyond an obvious high, you're part of the liquidity. Invalidation should be based on where the idea is genuinely wrong, not simply the nearest swing.

**Assuming every equal high will be taken immediately.** Liquidity is a draw, not a deadline. Price can leave it untouched for a long time.

**Trading the sweep alone.** A sweep without a confirmed shift is an assumption. Waiting for confirmation means missing some moves — that's the cost of filtering out many more bad ones.

## Final thought

Thinking in liquidity changes how you see a chart. Old highs and lows stop being "support and resistance" and become pools of orders that price may seek out. Combined with structure, it gives you a clearer, more objective way to frame each trade — not certainty, but context.
