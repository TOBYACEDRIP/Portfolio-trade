Every trading framework I respect starts in the same place: **structure**. Before order blocks, before fair value gaps, before any entry model, you need to know how price is currently being delivered. Get that wrong and every other tool points you in the wrong direction.

This article covers how I read structure, the mistakes I see most often, and how it fits into the first stage of the TOBYACE Model.

## What market structure actually is

Market structure is simply the sequence of swing highs and swing lows that price leaves behind.

- **Bullish structure** — price is making higher highs and higher lows.
- **Bearish structure** — price is making lower lows and lower highs.
- **Consolidation** — price is rotating inside a range without a clear sequence either way.

That's it. The difficulty isn't the definition — it's deciding *which* highs and lows matter. Zoom in far enough and every candle is a swing. Structure only becomes useful when you are consistent about the timeframe and the type of swing you're tracking.

## Swing points: be strict

A swing high is a candle with a lower high on either side of it. A swing low is a candle with a higher low on either side. I keep it that mechanical on purpose. The more discretion you allow in defining structure, the easier it becomes to see the trend you *want* to see.

> If two traders can't agree on where the last swing low is, neither of them has a structural edge.

## External and internal structure

On any timeframe there are two layers worth separating:

1. **External structure** — the major swing high and swing low that define the current dealing range.
2. **Internal structure** — the smaller swings that form *inside* that range as price moves between its extremes.

Internal structure shifts all the time. External structure shifts far less often, and those shifts carry far more weight. A lot of frustration comes from treating an internal break as if it were an external one.

## The dealing range, premium and discount

Once you've marked the external high and low, you have a **dealing range**. Split it at the 50% level:

- The upper half is **premium** — relatively expensive.
- The lower half is **discount** — relatively cheap.

In a bullish environment I'm interested in buying from discount, not chasing price in premium. In a bearish environment, the opposite. This single filter removes a large number of low-quality trades.

## Break of structure vs. a genuine shift

A **break of structure** in the direction of the trend is continuation — the expected behaviour. A **Market Structure Shift** is a break *against* the prevailing short-term direction, and it's one of the first signs that order flow may be changing.

Not every break counts. What I look for:

- **Displacement** — a decisive candle body closing through the swing point, not a wick that pokes through and retreats.
- **Context** — did the shift happen after price took liquidity, at a higher-timeframe point of interest?
- **Imbalance left behind** — a strong move usually leaves a fair value gap, which becomes a refinement area later.

A shift without context is just a break. A shift after a liquidity sweep into a higher-timeframe POI is information.

## How structure fits the TOBYACE Model

Structure is the backbone of the **Top-Down Analysis** stage. I read it from the top:

| Timeframe | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Weekly / Daily | What is the larger direction and dealing range? |
| 4H / 1H | Where is price inside that range — premium or discount? |
| 15m and below | Has price shifted at my point of interest? |

The higher timeframes give direction. The lower timeframes give confirmation. If they disagree, there's no trade — and that is an acceptable outcome.

## Common mistakes

- **Marking too many swings.** Pick a timeframe and be consistent.
- **Calling a reversal on one candle.** Wait for a close and, ideally, displacement.
- **Ignoring the higher timeframe.** A bullish shift on the five-minute chart inside a bearish daily range is a counter-trend idea, and should be treated with more caution.
- **Trading the middle of the range.** Equilibrium is where conviction is lowest.

## Final thought

Structure doesn't tell you what price *will* do. It tells you what price *is* doing, which is far more useful. Everything else in the framework — liquidity, POIs, the entry model — is built on top of reading it honestly.
